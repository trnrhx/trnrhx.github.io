# The next twelve months of accelerated AI, and how regular people can position themselves

At the end of its November 5, 2026 audit of Anthropic, the evaluation group METR addressed the question that matters most for anyone planning more than six months ahead: what is going to slow this down? After reviewing Anthropic's internal data, METR stated that it found no evidence of significant bottlenecks in algorithms, training compute, experiment compute, or data that would prevent further capability progress over the next twelve months.

Yesterday, on November 11, 2026, researchers at the AI Futures Project plugged METR's new audit numbers into their industry forecasting models. They found that AI capability progress had already reached a speed in early November 2026 that their baseline scenario had not expected until April 2027, hitting an acceleration threshold that carried less than a 4 percent probability in forecasts made just ninety days ago.

Combining the automated reinforcement learning loop from Part 1 with the split between commercial software and classified frontier models from Part 2 maps out the trajectory between November 2026 and November 2027. Tracing where this digital speedup hits physical and legal bottlenecks shows where regular people can position their careers and capital.

### What happens between November 2026 and November 2027

![Anthropic R&D Automation Trajectory showing the share of model R&D tasks shifting to AL4 AI leads and AL5 Fully automated across 2026 and 2027](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!sFvS!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F26315143-62b0-4880-a80e-a7eea616a681_1200x722.jpeg)

Labs will close the outer research loop and saturate every standard benchmark by spring 2027. Right now, human engineers at Anthropic still read the results of their automated `[agent-scaffold]` experiments and type in the next batch of ideas. With `Model 4` already speeding up experiment execution by up to 12x, the human researcher reading charts is now the slowest piece of the machinery. Over the next three to four months, labs will hand that outer step to the models as well, letting `Model 4` and `Model 5` read the overnight training logs and automatically queue the next round of experiments. As the 30-day checkpoint cycle continues, standard coding, math, and science benchmarks will saturate past 90 percent accuracy by mid-2027. To stop models from gaming their own automated tests, labs will anchor their training loops in strict mathematical proof checkers (such as the programming language Lean 4), hardware speed tests where models rewrite computer chip software, and live cybersecurity sandboxes.

At the same time, the gap between public AI tools and internal lab models will widen to roughly two historical years. Because labs cannot afford to pull megawatts of electricity off their internal self-improvement loops or let competitors copy their newest models through public APIs, they will hold their flagship checkpoints back much longer. If a lab holds its best internal model back for nine months while internal capabilities are advancing at 2.5x the historical pace, the public AI subscription you use at work in late 2027 will sit nearly two historical years behind what is running inside the lab's secure servers. Public models will still get cheaper and more reliable at routine office tasks, while the bleeding edge disappears from public view.

That split will produce sudden leaps in clean, verifiable domains alongside stubborn delays in the physical world. As METR noted in its audit, automated reinforcement learning thrives on problems that a computer can grade in milliseconds. Over the next year, expect rapid breakthroughs in formal mathematics (where internal models have already begun resolving longstanding Millennium Prize problems), computer chip layout, compiler optimization, and automated cyber offense. Conversely, any problem where the grade requires waiting on physical biology, human institutions, or material wear and tear (such as proving a newly designed drug is safe in human Phase 2 clinical trials or getting a county board to approve a power substation) will still move at human speed.

Finally, the late-November 2026 legal definition of "Super Intelligence" will lock the top tier inside defense frameworks. Once the White House Science Advisor submits the new statutory definition required by Executive Order 14434 at the end of this month, the Director of National Intelligence's Super Intelligence Force will have the legal hook to classify internal checkpoints that cross specific R&D and cyber thresholds. Throughout 2027, the frontier teams at Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google will increasingly operate under security clearances inside shielded facilities as sovereign intelligence and research contractors.

### Why buying Big Tech stocks is an overcrowded, flawed bet

![Big Tech Capital Expenditures in billions of USD from 2019 to 2027 estimates showing spending reaching 2.2 percent of US GDP](https://www.carsongroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/blog-2026-02-hyperscalar-capex-scaled.png)

When people hear that AI capability growth has doubled, their first instinct is to buy stock in Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, or wait for Anthropic's upcoming public offering. That trade is already priced in by every institutional desk on Wall Street, and it carries two major risks.

According to S&P Global projections, the six largest cloud hyperscalers are on track to spend more than $1.3 trillion on AI data centers and hardware by 2027, a capital spending binge so large that several of them are projected to run negative free operating cash flow while buying chips that depreciate in three years.

Furthermore, if the federal government fences off the most powerful internal models under national security rules while cheap open-source and distilled models turn everyday business AI into a low-margin utility, public equity shareholders may end up funding a trillion-dollar infrastructure buildout while the sovereign state and specialized patent partners capture the richest upside.

### How to position your career and business: own the verification and physical choke points

![Anthropic workforce data showing junior technical hires falling from 27 percent in early 2025 to 9 percent in 2026](https://i0.wp.com/www.saastr.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/c889b669a2e69b947063ce5196adcc782037bccefe0fa0be91526846c0ed9819.jpg?fit=750%2C582&quality=70&ssl=1)

On October 16, 2026, Anthropic's internal economics team published a study examining how work inside their own company had changed now that active AI agent hours outnumber human employee hours 6 to 1. Their findings preview how technical and professional work will reorganize over the next twelve months. Demand for junior implementation roles (people hired simply to write routine code or draft first-pass documents) dropped sharply. At the same time, the scarcest resource inside the building became human verification and judgment. Staff reported spending up to half their work week auditing, grading, and verifying the flood of work produced by their AI agents.

Four practical career and business strategies follow from that bottleneck.

Licensed professionals can act as the statutory verifier and legal liability sink. As AI drives the cost of generating raw technical drafts (software code, patent disclosures, legal briefs, architectural plans, and tax filings) down toward zero, the volume of submissions hitting regulatory agencies will surge. However, the US Patent and Trademark Office, the FDA, federal courts, municipal building departments, and malpractice insurers cannot put a software container in prison or hold an AI model legally liable for fraud or negligence. Someone with a statutory license (a patent practitioner, attorney, professional engineer, CPA, or licensed clinician) holds a legal monopoly on the final signature. A licensed professional who builds an automated auditing workflow to verify AI-generated drafts can handle the output volume of a fifteen-person firm with minimal overhead, charging clients for legal sign-off and verification rather than hours spent typing.

Operators without a statutory license can build grading harnesses and audit pipelines for mid-sized businesses. Outside of clean software unit tests, most companies have no reliable way to measure whether an AI agent is subtly fabricating numbers in their accounting, legal discovery, or supply chain data. Building the verification rules, test datasets, and automated cross-checks that catch AI errors before they reach a client or regulator is far more defensible than selling generic AI wrappers.

On the physical side, business owners can control un-scrapable, hyper-local operations and data. A classified supercomputer running inside a Virginia SCIF can solve partial differential equations, yet it knows nothing about the physical state of a local municipality, a coastal marine corridor, a regional construction market, or a local service route unless someone on the ground owns the relationships and physical assets. Local physical service businesses, specialized trades, local real estate entitlements, and hyper-local information platforms backed by on-the-ground verification cannot be distilled out of an AI model's weights. By using cheap commercial AI to automate 90 percent of scheduling, dispatch, data ingestion, and back-office paperwork, an operator can run a traditional local business or local data asset with software-like profit margins inside a moat that frontier labs cannot cross.

Technical consultants can also offer private, self-hosted AI infrastructure for clients who cannot touch the public cloud. As federal monitoring expands over major cloud providers under the Super Intelligence accords, law firms, intellectual property practices, medical groups, defense subcontractors, and family offices will refuse to send sensitive client secrets to public APIs. Deploying and maintaining self-hosted, open-weight models and private databases on local hardware lets you sell air-gapped automation to high-margin clients who are legally barred from using public cloud chatbots.

### Where to deploy investment capital: buy the physical tollbooths

Looking at where the $1.3 trillion data center buildout and the flood of computer-generated inventions get stuck entering the physical world points to four hardware and real-asset bottlenecks.

High-voltage power grid hardware and thermal cooling sit at the front of the line. An internal AI loop can optimize its software in thirty days, allowing a lab to run millions of automated training agents around the clock. Running those agents requires gigawatts of continuous electricity. The tightest bottlenecks in the electric grid sit in step-up transformers, high-voltage switchgear, grain-oriented electrical steel, copper interconnects, and liquid-cooling loops for server racks, all of which carry multi-year manufacturing backlogs. Mid-cap industrial companies that build electrical grid hardware and data center thermal systems collect revenue from every megawatt added to the grid.

Wet-lab contract research organizations (CROs) and lab automation hardware capture the biological bottleneck. When Anthropic (through Coefficient Bio) and Google DeepMind (through Isomorphic Labs) use their internal models to generate ten thousand promising drug candidates on a computer screen, every single candidate hits the same physical wall: it must be synthesized in a wet lab, tested on living cells and animal models, and run through multi-year FDA human clinical trials. The AI labs do not own global networks of clinical trial hospitals or warehouses of mass spectrometers and liquid-handling robots. Contract research organizations and automated lab-equipment manufacturers act as the physical tollbooth for AI drug discovery, getting paid on the volume of molecules tested regardless of which single drug eventually wins approval.

Cleared defense IT integrators and secure facility contractors capture the federal classification wave. Moving frontier models into classified national security workflows under Executive Order 14434 requires building physical SCIF server rooms, wiring air-gapped government cloud networks, and supplying thousands of engineers who already hold TS/SCI security clearances. Mid-tier defense technology integrators that already hold facility clearances and multi-year intelligence contracts are positioned to capture the federal spending that follows the late-November statutory rules.

Finally, scarce physical assets benefit from digital deflation. When autonomous software drives down the cost of digital content, routine code, and white-collar desk work, capital flows toward assets that cannot be synthesized inside a data center: debt-free land in supply-constrained markets, cash-flowing local physical infrastructure, and hard commodities.

Before committing your time or capital to any venture over the next twelve months, apply a single test: ask what happens to the value of what you own if 100,000 AI agents run overnight for free. If those agents can produce what you sell, your margins are heading to zero. If those 100,000 agents need your high-voltage transformer, your wet-lab testing equipment, your local physical property, or your licensed legal signature before their output can enter the real world, they are working overnight to drive demand straight to your door.
